Coverage

Fellow & resident physician disability insurance: key terms

Plain-language context for physician disability insurance topics for trainees, including guaranteed standard issue (GSI) and true own-occupation—plus how your medical specialty factors in. Ask Jay what applies in your case.

PGY1 helps you compare specialty-specific and true own-occupation individual disability options with programs your institution may offer, including guaranteed standard issue (GSI) where available. Plan design, rates, and underwriting depend on the carrier, your training program, and how you apply. The questionnaire starts a broker conversation, not a binding quote.

GSI (guaranteed standard issue)

A group- or program-linked way eligible trainees at some programs may access disability coverage, subject to program and carrier rules. Not identical to all individually underwritten plans.

No health questions (when applicable)

Some GSI and similar programs are set up so that eligible participants are not required to complete personal health questions the same way as a traditional private application; eligibility and rules vary by class and carrier.

Pre-existing conditions

The impact of prior health history depends on the product path (GSI, group, or private underwriting) and the carrier’s requirements. A broker can explain reasonable expectations; this is not a promise of a specific outcome.

Frequently asked questions

What is GSI (guaranteed standard issue)?

Guaranteed standard issue, or GSI, refers to disability insurance programs that may be offered to eligible members of a class (for example, through a hospital or program), often with a simplified path compared with full individual medical underwriting, subject to the carrier’s and employer’s rules. Availability and details depend on your program and carrier. Jay can explain which options, if any, you may be eligible to explore.

What is true own-occupation coverage?

True own-occupation and related own-occ definitions describe how a policy may define total disability, often in relation to your ability to work in your medical specialty. Wording, riders, and benefits vary by policy. The questionnaire and follow-up help match you to options to discuss with your broker—this page is general education, not a policy document.

How does physician disability insurance relate to my medical specialty?

For physicians, disability insurance is often shaped around protecting income in the specialty you are training for. The questionnaire covers your training stage, program, and goals so a broker can align options with your medical specialty and career path. Specific definitions come from the policy you choose.

Do GSI or similar programs require health questions?

Eligibility and underwriting rules depend on the specific GSI or group program, your institution, and carrier requirements. Some programs are designed so that qualified participants are not required to complete personal health questions the same way as a traditional private application, but eligibility and rules vary. A broker can confirm what applies in your case.

What about pre-existing conditions?

The impact of prior health history depends on the product path you use (GSI, group, or private underwriting) and the carrier’s forms. A broker can explain reasonable expectations for your situation; this site does not guarantee an offer, rate, or terms.
