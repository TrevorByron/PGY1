# Match Day checklist: from MS4 to PGY1

**Jay Weinberg, CLU, ChFC** From his Spring 2026 webinar for graduating medical students · Updated October 30, 2026

How to use it

Rank these by your own situation and work through them one at a time. For most people, a student loan plan and disability insurance come first.

A note from Jay

I'm not a student loan specialist. If your situation is complicated, or before you make any definitive moves on your loans, meet with your financial aid office or a student loan specialist.

## Taxes and student loans

- **File your taxes as an MS4, even with no income** Loan servicers use your tax return to verify income. Filing speeds things up and can set up a $0 payment for intern year. [Why file with no income](https://www.pgy1.com/questions/file-taxes-as-ms4/)
- **Talk to financial aid or a student loan specialist before any big move** Jay's not a student loan specialist, and this year is different from past years. Get advice on your situation first. [Direct consolidation before residency?](https://www.pgy1.com/questions/direct-consolidation-before-residency/)
- **Pick a repayment plan** For most PGY1s it comes down to IBR or RAP. In residency the monthly payments are usually close. [IBR or RAP](https://www.pgy1.com/questions/ibr-vs-rap-for-residents/)
- **Going for PSLF? Certify every year** Right loans, right employer, 120 qualifying payments. Annual certification keeps your count clean. [What PSLF requires](https://www.pgy1.com/questions/pslf-requirements/)

## Disability insurance

- **Check whether your hospital has a GSI policy, before you apply for anything** A GSI policy has no health questions. Applying for an underwritten policy first can knock you out of it. [Look up your hospital](https://www.pgy1.com/gsi/)
- **Learn the one feature that matters most: own-occupation** The definition of disability decides whether a policy pays. Not every policy has true own-occupation wording. [True own-occupation](https://www.pgy1.com/questions/own-occupation-disability-insurance/)
- **Budget about $100 a month** Jay's rule of thumb for a resident or fellow around 30. Start small and increase later. [What it costs](https://www.pgy1.com/questions/disability-insurance-cost-resident/)

## Moving and mail

- **Update your mailing address** Student loan servicers, credit card companies and anywhere else important. Then forward your mail.
- **Put every bill you can on autopay** A missed bill that lands at your old address can end up on your credit report.
- **Read your mail** You'll be extremely busy. Carve out a few minutes a week so nothing slips.

## Your first paycheck

- **Work out your take-home pay and build a budget** Rent or mortgage, car, insurance, food, parking. A spreadsheet or the back of a napkin. Don't spend more than you make. [Budget and emergency fund](https://www.pgy1.com/questions/budget-and-emergency-fund-intern-year/)
- **Start an emergency fund** Roughly three months of living expenses, in money you can get to on short notice. [How much to set aside](https://www.pgy1.com/questions/budget-and-emergency-fund-intern-year/)
- **Look at a Roth IRA** After-tax money in, tax-free money out. Eligibility depends on income and filing status. Talk to an investment professional. [Roth IRA as a resident](https://www.pgy1.com/questions/roth-ira-as-a-resident/)

## Depending on your situation

- **Married, have dependents or a co-signer on your loans? Look at life insurance** Those are the usual triggers. Most people Jay works with choose level-premium term. [Life insurance as a resident](https://www.pgy1.com/questions/life-insurance-as-a-resident/)
- **Rent or buy** How many years is your training, and where did you match? Most residents are renting right now. [Rent or buy](https://www.pgy1.com/questions/rent-or-buy-during-residency/)
- **Thinking about refinancing? Probably not if you want PSLF** Once loans leave the government you can't put them back. The exception is high-rate private loans. [Private refinance](https://www.pgy1.com/questions/private-refinance-student-loans/)
