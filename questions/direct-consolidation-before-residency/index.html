Should I do a direct consolidation before residency?

Jay Weinberg, CLU, ChFCPhysician disability insurance specialist · PGY1 Financial Solutions

Short answer

Not necessarily, this year. In past years the usual advice was to consolidate right away to start the clock on PSLF. In 2026, a consolidation that finishes processing after the new RAP plan launches in July may leave RAP as your only option, when you might want IBR. Talk to financial aid before you do it.

A note from Jay

I'm not a student loan specialist. This reflects my update from March 2026, and student loan rules are changing quickly. Before you make a move, talk with your school's financial aid office or a student loan specialist.

What changed

A direct consolidation takes all your loans and lumps them into one big loan with a weighted interest rate. In past years, the advice would likely have been to do one and get the clock going toward public service loan forgiveness as quickly as possible.

We're in an entirely different landscape now. A new program called RAP rolls out in July 2026. If you consolidate now and it takes a few months to process, and it gets processed after July, RAP may be the only option for you, when you might actually want to be in IBR (income-based repayment).

If you're leaning toward IBR

You may want to consider waiting out your grace period. Connect with your financial aid department or a student loan specialist, bring them up to speed on your situation, and they should be able to weigh in on the best play.
