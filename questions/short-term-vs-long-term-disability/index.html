What's the difference between short-term and long-term disability insurance?

Jay Weinberg, CLU, ChFCPhysician disability insurance specialist · PGY1 Financial SolutionsUpdated October 30, 2026

Short answer

Short-term policies typically kick in after you've been out of work a week or two and may pay for a few months. Long-term policies typically kick in after a few months out of work, and as long as you keep meeting the policy's definition of disability, you may be able to collect until retirement age.

Short-term

Short-term policies typically kick in when you're out of work for a week or two, and they may pay out benefits for a few months.

Long-term

Long-term policies typically don't kick in until you're out of work for a few months, often three or six. Assuming you can continue to trip that litmus test, the policy's definition of total disability, you may be able to collect until retirement age.

That's why the definition of disability matters so much on a long-term policy. It's the test you have to keep meeting for as long as you collect.
