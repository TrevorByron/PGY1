# How should I budget and build an emergency fund in intern year?

**Jay Weinberg, CLU, ChFC** Physician disability insurance specialist · PGY1 Financial Solutions · Updated October 30, 2026

Short answer

Start from your take-home pay after taxes and insurance, list what you have to pay, and don't spend more than you make. Set aside what's left as an emergency fund, typically give or take three months of living expenses you can reach on short notice.

## Build the budget from take-home pay

As a medical student you probably went four years without a paycheck. Now you're getting one. Figure out roughly what's left after taxes, insurance and everything else comes out. Then work out what you can put toward rent or a mortgage, a car payment and insurance, food and parking. A spreadsheet or the back of a napkin both work.

The last thing you want to do is spend more than you make, especially without savings, family help or a side hustle.

## The emergency fund

Set whatever's extra aside for an emergency fund. Everyone has their own definition, but typically it's give or take three months of living expenses, in money you can access on short notice.

## When you move

- Update your mailing address with your student loan servicers, credit card companies and other important places, and forward your mail.
- Put every bill you can on autopay. A missed bill can come back to bite you as a hit on your credit report.
- Read your mail. You'll be extremely busy, but carve out a few minutes a week.
