How much does disability insurance cost for a resident or fellow?

Jay Weinberg, CLU, ChFCPhysician disability insurance specialist · PGY1 Financial SolutionsUpdated October 30, 2026

Short answer

As a budgeting rule of thumb, around $100 a month, give or take, should get a resident or fellow around 30 years old a foot in the door on a policy. It can be less or more depending on your situation. This isn't a quote for any one person.

A number to budget around

This isn't pricing for any one person. But I always tell residents and fellows: if you can carve out about $100 a month, that's likely going to get you into the ballpark for a policy with the right wording and features. Could it be $50 or $75? Absolutely, in certain situations. Could it be $150 or $200? Also yes.

Most graduating medical students are somewhere between 25 and 30. I wouldn't be surprised if many could get a foot in the door for $40, $50 or $60 a month.

Start small, increase later

The idea is to get a low-cost, low-coverage policy now that puts the insurance company on the hook to give you more coverage later, without new health questions. That's the future increase option.

Be wary of prices that look too good

Not every policy costs the same, but if one is an extreme outlier, chances are something's off. Agents can make pricing look lower by gutting a policy, leaving out features, or showing pricing that isn't guaranteed.
