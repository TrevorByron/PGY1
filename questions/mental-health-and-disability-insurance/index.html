Can past mental health treatment affect getting disability insurance?

Jay Weinberg, CLU, ChFCPhysician disability insurance specialist · PGY1 Financial SolutionsUpdated October 30, 2026

Short answer

Yes, with a medically underwritten policy it will come up. It's the most common item I see affect approval. If you have treatment or counseling in your records, check whether a GSI policy is available, because it will likely give you wider coverage.

What happens in medical underwriting

The insurance company asks about your current and past health and pre-existing medical and psychological conditions. It looks at insurance billing codes and runs a pharmacy database check for prescriptions in your name. It's very strict about prescriptions with no chart notes behind them, like something you self-prescribed or a friend or attending prescribed for you.

There are three general outcomes. Best case, you get a policy that covers everything, which happens, but not that often. Worst case, a postpone or decline. And in the middle, you get a policy with modifications, ratings or exclusions: certain conditions or body parts carved out, a higher price, or both.

The number one item

The number one item I see is current or past treatment for mental health: anxiety, stress, depression, ADHD. Medical school is stressful. You had to interview, match and pass a lot of tests, and COVID was hard on everyone in health care. A lot of medical students, residents and fellows have sought treatment. In my experience, over 50% have something in their file that will affect their ability to get a clean policy.

Where GSI fits

A GSI policy doesn't ask health questions, so it will likely offer a wider scope of coverage if this applies to you. Most GSI policies do cover mental health, but cap how long you can collect for it, typically at 24 months.
