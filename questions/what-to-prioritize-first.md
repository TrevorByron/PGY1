# Disability insurance, student loans or a Roth IRA: what should I do first?

**Jay Weinberg, CLU, ChFC** Physician disability insurance specialist · PGY1 Financial Solutions · Updated October 30, 2026

Short answer

Rank the topics for your own situation and work down the list. For most people, a game plan for student loans and securing disability insurance end up at the top. Retirement savings come after those.

## The pecking order

We usually cover student loans, disability insurance, buying a house, life insurance, saving for retirement, an emergency fund and a budget. I'd encourage you to rank them one through seven and peck away at them based on your situation. For most people, a game plan on student loans and securing disability insurance are at the top of the list.

## Why disability insurance comes before a Roth

Say a resident has been putting $5,000 a year into a Roth IRA for two years, so there's $10,000 in it, and has no disability policy. If their health changes and they can't work, how long can they live off $10,000? Two, three, four months.

Now say they put $4,000 into the Roth and $1,000 toward a disability policy instead. Two years later, if something happens, they still have $8,000 to draw on, plus a stream of income payments to keep the lights on.

Everyone's circumstances are different, so base your plan on your own situation.
