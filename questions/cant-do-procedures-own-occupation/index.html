What if I can't do procedures anymore but can still practice medicine?

Jay Weinberg, CLU, ChFCPhysician disability insurance specialist · PGY1 Financial SolutionsUpdated October 30, 2026

Short answer

It depends on your policy and the wording within it. Some policies say that if you can't do your procedures or your hands-on patient care, you can receive benefits even if you stay in the same field of medicine. Others only pay if you're not working another job.

An example

Let's say you do three years of internal medicine, three years of general cardiology, and a couple of years after that for cath or EP. What happens if something happens to your hand and you can't do your procedures, but you can still be a general cardiologist, or you can still do outpatient internal medicine or work as a hospitalist?

It comes down to the wording

The answer is it really depends on your policy and the wording within that policy. There are certain policies that say if you can't do your procedures, you can't do your hands-on patient care, you're going to be able to receive benefits even if you stay in your same field of medicine. There are other policies that are very general or vague, where they say if you can't do your job, we're only going to pay you if you're not doing another job. And there are other policies where if you get another job, they may pay you the difference in that income loss.

Own occupation isn't the same everywhere

If I showed you five policies from five different companies, they'd all have different wording for own occupation. It's specific to the policy and specific to the company. The highest level is medical specialty specific own occupation, and if you're heavily weighted toward procedures, you may want that accounted for in your policy.
