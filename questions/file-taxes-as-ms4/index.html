Should I file taxes as a fourth-year medical student with no income?

Jay Weinberg, CLU, ChFCPhysician disability insurance specialist · PGY1 Financial SolutionsUpdated October 30, 2026

Short answer

Yes. When your loans come due, servicers link to your tax return to verify income. A return showing no income speeds things up and can set you up for a $0 payment in your PGY1 year. Without one, they may ask for a pay stub, which will show income.

A note from Jay

I'm not a student loan specialist. This reflects my update from March 2026, and student loan rules are changing quickly. Before you make a move, talk with your school's financial aid office or a student loan specialist.

Why file with no income

When you engage your loans, the student loan servicers link up your tax return to verify your income. As a fourth-year medical student you likely have no income. Filing speeds the process along, and it will likely set the stage for a $0 payment for your PGY1 year.

What happens if you don't

If you don't file, they may require a pay stub or some other financial verification. A pay stub will naturally show some income, which may create a situation where you have a payment as a PGY1. So do your taxes, even if you don't have any income as a fourth-year.
