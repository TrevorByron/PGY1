# Should I open a Roth IRA as a resident?

**Jay Weinberg, CLU, ChFC** Physician disability insurance specialist · PGY1 Financial Solutions · Updated October 30, 2026

Short answer

It can be a wonderful retirement vehicle while you're earning resident money and expect to earn attending money later. You put in after-tax dollars, it grows tax-deferred, and it comes out tax-free. Eligibility depends on your income and tax filing status.

## How it works

After-tax in, grows tax-deferred, comes out tax-free on the back side. That's especially useful when you're making resident money now and fully expect to make attending money later.

## Who's eligible

Your eligibility to put money into a Roth IRA depends on your income and your tax filing status. An investment professional can tell you what you're eligible for and help you come up with a game plan.

## Where it fits

Some people view the money they put into a Roth IRA, as opposed to earnings, as part of their emergency fund, because contributions may be able to come out without a penalty. Find out what you could take out if something comes up before you count on it.

For most residents, a student loan plan and disability insurance come before retirement savings.
