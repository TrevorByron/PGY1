# Disability insurance questions from residents and fellows

Short answers first, then Jay's full explanation from his webinars, with the video cued to the moment he answers it.

## Before Match

For MS4s and anyone about to start residency.

- [Should I get disability insurance before residency starts? · Not before you've done three things: find out where you matched, find out whether there's a GSI policy at that hospital, and find an insurance professional who can offer you both the GSI policy and a medically underwritten one.](https://www.pgy1.com/questions/disability-insurance-before-residency/)
- [How much does disability insurance cost for a resident or fellow? · As a budgeting rule of thumb, around $100 a month, give or take, should get a resident or fellow around 30 years old a foot in the door on a policy.](https://www.pgy1.com/questions/disability-insurance-cost-resident/)
- [Is disability insurance more expensive in California? · Usually, yes. Insurance pricing and wording are regulated state by state, and California is the state that stands out for higher pricing, modified policies, or both.](https://www.pgy1.com/questions/disability-insurance-california/)

## GSI policies

Guaranteed standard issue: the no-health-questions option many teaching hospitals have.

- [What is a GSI disability insurance policy? · GSI stands for guaranteed standard issue. It's a disability policy offered to residents and fellows at certain teaching hospitals that doesn't ask health questions.](https://www.pgy1.com/questions/what-is-gsi-disability-insurance/)
- [Should I apply for a medically underwritten policy before checking for GSI? · No. Most GSI policies ask whether you've previously applied for disability insurance, and if you have, there's a good chance you won't be eligible.](https://www.pgy1.com/questions/apply-underwritten-before-gsi/)
- [Can past mental health treatment affect getting disability insurance? · Yes, with a medically underwritten policy it will come up. It's the most common item I see affect approval.](https://www.pgy1.com/questions/mental-health-and-disability-insurance/)
- [What are the eligibility questions and limits of a GSI policy? · Guardian's GSI asks whether you've applied for disability insurance before, whether you owe active-duty military time, whether you're collecting disability benefits from another source, and whether you've been actively at work for the past 180 days.](https://www.pgy1.com/questions/gsi-eligibility-and-limitations/)
- [I already applied for a medically underwritten policy. Can I still get GSI? · Maybe. Having applied is the knockout question that disqualifies the most people, so there's a high likelihood you won't be eligible.](https://www.pgy1.com/questions/already-applied-can-i-still-get-gsi/)
- [Do GSI disability policies cost more? · Not the ones I frequently come across. With the Guardian GSI, and with other companies too, there are no price increases or surcharges for getting the policy without health questions.](https://www.pgy1.com/questions/do-gsi-policies-cost-more/)
- [Why can't my GME office tell me if there's a GSI policy? · Because GSI policies are individual policies, not part of your hospital's benefits package.](https://www.pgy1.com/questions/why-gme-cant-tell-you-about-gsi/)
- [How do I tell a strong GSI policy from a scaled-back one? · Check the definition of disability, the knockout questions, whether there's pre-existing conditions wording, the level of riders offered, the insurance company's financial strength, and whether it has a future increase option.](https://www.pgy1.com/questions/how-to-compare-gsi-policies/)
- [Can I wait until I finish training to get a GSI policy? · I wouldn't. These policies aren't guaranteed to exist in the future, and the insurance company can end the offer at any time, usually without advance notice.](https://www.pgy1.com/questions/wait-until-end-of-training-for-gsi/)
- [Does a GSI policy cover hobbies like scuba diving or rock climbing? · The GSI policies I'm talking about overlook pre-existing conditions and hazardous activities.](https://www.pgy1.com/questions/gsi-hazardous-activities/)
- [Can I get disability insurance as a resident with type 1 diabetes? · It depends on who you go to. If you go to an insurance professional that can't offer you a GSI policy, it's highly unlikely you'd be able to get a policy.](https://www.pgy1.com/questions/disability-insurance-type-1-diabetes/)
- [My hospital isn't on the GSI list. Could there still be a GSI policy? · Possibly. If your hospital isn't listed, that does not mean a special disability policy doesn't exist.](https://www.pgy1.com/questions/hospital-not-on-gsi-list/)

## Choosing a policy

The wording and features that decide whether a policy actually pays.

- [What is medical-specialty-specific true own-occupation disability insurance? · It's the strongest definition of disability a policy can have.](https://www.pgy1.com/questions/own-occupation-disability-insurance/)
- [Is the group disability insurance from my employer enough? · Usually not on its own. Group policies are often taxable, tied to your job and capped, and many use "any gainful occupation" wording.](https://www.pgy1.com/questions/group-disability-insurance-enough/)
- [What is the future increase option on a disability policy? · It lets you increase your coverage later, for example when you start earning attending pay, without answering health questions.](https://www.pgy1.com/questions/future-increase-option/)
- [How do I choose a disability insurance agent? · Work with someone who specializes in physician disability insurance and can offer both GSI and medically underwritten policies.](https://www.pgy1.com/questions/choosing-a-disability-insurance-agent/)
- [How much disability coverage can I get as an attending? · Most physicians I work with are eligible for somewhere between $10,000 and $15,000 a month, depending on income and whether your employer gives you a group policy.](https://www.pgy1.com/questions/how-much-disability-coverage-attending/)
- [What's the difference between short-term and long-term disability insurance? · Short-term policies typically kick in after you've been out of work a week or two and may pay for a few months.](https://www.pgy1.com/questions/short-term-vs-long-term-disability/)
- [What happens in medical underwriting, and can an exclusion be removed later? · The insurer reviews your health history, medical records, prescriptions, billing codes and family history, then either covers you for everything, postpones or declines, or offers a policy with exclusions, ratings or modifications.](https://www.pgy1.com/questions/medical-underwriting-exclusions/)
- [Should I price shop disability insurance? · No. The stronger policies are naturally going to cost a bit more than the scaled-back ones.](https://www.pgy1.com/questions/price-shopping-disability-insurance/)
- [A friend or relative sells insurance. Should I use them for disability insurance? · Not just because they sell insurance. Very few insurance professionals specialize in helping physicians, and even fewer have access to the exclusive GSI policies.](https://www.pgy1.com/questions/friend-or-family-who-sells-insurance/)

## Medical school to residency

Student loans, taxes, housing and your first paycheck, from Jay's spring transition webinar.

- [Disability insurance, student loans or a Roth IRA: what should I do first? · Rank the topics for your own situation and work down the list.](https://www.pgy1.com/questions/what-to-prioritize-first/)
- [Should I file taxes as a fourth-year medical student with no income? · Yes. When your loans come due, servicers link to your tax return to verify income.](https://www.pgy1.com/questions/file-taxes-as-ms4/)
- [Should I do a direct consolidation before residency? · Not necessarily, this year. In past years the usual advice was to consolidate right away to start the clock on PSLF.](https://www.pgy1.com/questions/direct-consolidation-before-residency/)
- [IBR or RAP: which repayment plan should I pick as a resident? · For most graduating students these are the two options, and as a resident the monthly payments won't differ much.](https://www.pgy1.com/questions/ibr-vs-rap-for-residents/)
- [What do I need to qualify for Public Service Loan Forgiveness? · Three boxes: the right kind of loans (government direct loans), the right kind of employer (government or nonprofit 501(c)(3)), and 120 qualifying payments.](https://www.pgy1.com/questions/pslf-requirements/)
- [Should I refinance my student loans with a private lender as a resident? · Rarely, right now. Refinancing government loans with a private lender permanently gives up PSLF and benefits like RAP's interest subsidy, and private rates may not be much lower today.](https://www.pgy1.com/questions/private-refinance-student-loans/)
- [Should I rent or buy during residency? · It depends on how long your training is, where you matched, and what buying costs compared with renting there.](https://www.pgy1.com/questions/rent-or-buy-during-residency/)
- [Should I open a Roth IRA as a resident? · It can be a wonderful retirement vehicle while you're earning resident money and expect to earn attending money later.](https://www.pgy1.com/questions/roth-ira-as-a-resident/)
- [How should I budget and build an emergency fund in intern year? · Start from your take-home pay after taxes and insurance, list what you have to pay, and don't spend more than you make.](https://www.pgy1.com/questions/budget-and-emergency-fund-intern-year/)
